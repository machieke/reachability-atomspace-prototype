# Codex handoff — Frozen integrated-loop transfer evaluation

## Decision

Close the payload experiment at publication `d50baa1`. End the native-runtime and payload optimization sequence. Do not make native retrieval outperform scanning a prerequisite for further cognitive research.

The next increment is a bounded evaluation of the existing integrated controller on newly declared task structures. It is not a new scheduler, a storage optimization, a pressure experiment, or a production release.

This is a proposed next protocol. No new transfer episodes or results have been produced by this handoff.

## 1. Preserved evidence and baseline

Preserve the payload study's measured implementation/auditor `28f9dad3543de46725121f8b48079d020991e566`, its preregistration, original full and compact implementations, native build receipts, test inventories, failure records, publications, and archive.

The published payload result is a 62.89% load-wire reduction and a 60.04% readback-wire reduction, with unchanged supported semantics. Aggregate episode times were 241.274257 seconds compact, 241.020144 seconds full-session native, and 149.839596 seconds scan. This establishes no aggregate elapsed-time improvement. Do not reinterpret transfer-byte totals as RSS, peak-memory measurements, or cognitive gains.

Keep the independently recorded eight phase-command exits of zero and unresolved outer-shell exit 143 separate. Do not invent a cause or repeat completed frozen suites merely to obtain a different wrapper receipt.

Before implementation, record the actual controller, world, rule/decision policies, capture interfaces, query backend, native builds, and auditor identities. Publication hashes and measured source hashes must remain distinguishable.

## 2. Research question

Can the unchanged integrated loop acquire needed information, execute supported multi-hop numerical reasoning, preserve complete assessment and authority contracts, and recognize physical outcomes on new small task structures within its existing fragment?

Separate:

1. Contract conformance: no invalid operation, stale permission, fabricated evidence, omitted required review, or invented observation.
2. Task effectiveness: whether a task is completed, remains unresolved, or misses an achievable opportunity under the declared information and budgets.
3. Retrieval equivalence and engineering cost: whether scan and native discovery make the same semantic choices, and what each costs.

The scan arm is a retrieval comparator for the same cognitive policy, not an independent simpler reasoner. Agreement with it cannot establish cognitive superiority, the value of pressure, or the optimality of the controller.

The old six parent fixtures are inspected regression examples. Timing sweeps, formula modes, renamings, and backend repetitions do not create new independent task structures.

## 3. Freeze the controller

Use the existing multi-hop consumer without changing:

- Its registered deduction fragment, five ordered numerical premises, maximum supported reasoning depth, complete AND requirements, alternative routes, and exact revision binding.
- Its FIFO selection, control/report priorities, required review, attempted-work identity, budgets, and monitoring headroom.
- Numerical formulas, provenance/independence rules, local numerical certification scope, live all-current policy A, advisory obligation policy B, and explicit assessment roles.
- Evidence retirement, alternative-support handling, authorization, resources, executor semantics, product identity, health sampling, lifecycle history, and goal accounting.
- The observation-independent physical world, its clock semantics, and the separation of actual physical loss from recognized loss.

No precomputed intermediate beliefs or target conclusions may be supplied as observations. Registered models and legitimate input estimates are permitted; fixture answers masquerading as evidence are not.

If a new fixture reveals a genuine runtime defect, preserve the frozen result. Do not silently fix the controller and relabel the same cohort as an untouched evaluation. A correction requires a separately identified source and affected-result record. Do not expand the increment into generalized repair.

## 4. Two execution arms only

1. `TRANSFER-scan`: frozen scanner discovery, actual pinned-native PLN for selected numerical work.
2. `TRANSFER-native`: frozen full-session native recall, the same actual pinned-native PLN and consumer.

Use the full-session native arm for continuity with the established integrated reference, not as a claim that it is a better design than compact. Retain compact as a documented optional representation; do not rerun the full/compact or runtime-lifetime matrices.

Keep actual native retrieval distinct from native formula execution. Both arms execute selected formulas natively; only the discovery source differs.

Do not introduce native query-result caches, scanner rescue, persistent helpers, incremental native updates, new runtime packaging, or reduced snapshot checking.

## 5. Dataset: at most twelve new parent tasks

Create a bounded input set with twelve new parent structures in three families, four per family. This is a practical scope limit, not a sample-size calculation or a broad generalization claim.

### Family A — New dependency compositions

Use genuinely different supported two- and three-hop dependency arrangements: for example different placements of shared intermediates, complete alternative producers, and missing upstream reports.

Keep all source/model roles and required assessment semantics explicit. Preserve contrary-producer review. A real inference step is not a redundant Boolean wrapper.

### Family B — Information and work constraints

Use variations in available versus unavailable registered observation opportunities, eligible independent versus copied sources, and the work needed to finish an otherwise supported route.

The same public operation/interface semantics and original caps apply. Do not enlarge capacities until the chosen controller completes. Do not classify failure to finish under a budget as a safety violation unless an actual safety contract was violated.

### Family C — Changing evidence and outcomes

Use the existing supported mechanisms to change relevant support, replace an exact premise, or expose an adverse estimate. Reuse the existing independent-world events and measurement contracts to keep physical completion distinct from recognized completion.

Changes must be delivered through the normal public event/admission path. Hidden truth must not directly update authority. No new sensor model, latency semantics, execution-repair mechanism, or multi-action environment is needed.

### Cohort construction rules

- Keep every task inside the original rule, estimate, source, depth, query, snapshot, and world bounds. List those bounds in the protocol instead of quietly increasing them.
- Build the fixtures and label the intended semantic questions without running either frozen controller to choose favorable cases.
- Check task validity using independent structural checks and finite numerical checks. Where a shared probability interpretation is claimed, include an appropriate small fixture witness. Runtime per-invocation certification still does not become a global probabilistic-consistency guarantee.
- Preserve low-confidence, contrary, unavailable, and budget-limited cases. Do not resample evidence values until an execution passes.
- Commit/hash the complete cohort, roles, event schedules, budgets, and evaluator before measured execution.
- Renamed, reordered, and counterfactual siblings remain attached to their parent and are reported as related diagnostics, not independent new tasks.
- A parent discovered to be outside the declared fragment is retained as a construction/bound failure. Do not silently replace it after seeing controller outcomes.
- Any code required to express the cohort should be a thin test/input adapter using existing APIs, not a new runtime or general benchmark framework.

This is transfer within the existing declared fragment, not transfer to a new physical domain or evidence of general intelligence.

## 6. Public information and evaluation

Controllers may read actual public state, explicit task contracts, registered capabilities, their own previous receipts, and available observation opportunities. They must not receive future event scripts, evaluator labels, preferred action sequences, or hidden physical state.

The environment responds to selected requests and advances independently under its declared schedule. It must not drive the intended deduction sequence on the controller's behalf.

The evaluator may use hidden truth to evaluate physical outcomes. It must not treat hindsight knowledge as information the controller should have possessed.

Do not add a new general optimal planner. Use the existing independent reference components to check:

- Complete required inputs and role applicability.
- Local numerical admissibility and actual formula outputs.
- Current support, ancestry, invalidation, and execution authorization.
- Physical transitions and admitted-observation/lifecycle accounting.

Where a positive task has a feasibility witness, record the witness separately and establish the information assumptions it uses. A hindsight-feasible path is not automatically an implementable same-information policy. Only label a missed opportunity against a justified reference; otherwise record feasibility as unknown.

A conformance pass means the contracts were respected. It does not mean the task succeeded. An unresolved task is not automatically a correct abstention; retain the substantive reason and whether the reference establishes anything further.

## 7. Execution and parity

Run twelve parents across the two arms: twenty-four primary executions. Both arms use actual pinned-native PLN. Use deterministic finite calculations for independent verification and existing focused regressions; do not double the whole matrix solely for another execution count.

Use the same parent inputs, budgets, and exogenous schedules for both arms. Counterbalance their order across parents for descriptive timing; no further timing sweeps are required.

At matched public states and controller histories, compare semantic query answers, complete candidate applications, selected operations, exact inputs, certificates, observation requests, budgets, and stopping reasons. Raw native handles and backend-specific receipts need not be identical.

Compare final and time-resolved physical and recognized outcomes. If a parity mismatch occurs, retain both trajectories and find the earliest relevant divergence; do not simply report the final mismatch.

Use at most a small familiar positive/negative smoke check for wiring before the frozen cohort. Do not tune policies or query ordering using the new measured parents.

## 8. Report useful outcomes, not just passes

Publish one row per parent and arm with:

- Whether capture, review, and queries were complete.
- Accepted dependency depth and actual formula calls, including rejected or stale work.
- Physical completion, recognized completion, unresolved numerical/mandatory obligations, objections, and monitoring gaps.
- Stop classification: completed, no registered route, waiting/observation unavailable, live-policy blocked, incomplete/unsupported scope, or exhausted budget. Multiple simultaneous conditions must not be hidden.
- Physical and recognized loss trajectories/integrals under the unchanged clock semantics.
- Observations, requests, work consumed, exact replacements, repeated-work suppression, and authoritative effects.
- Snapshot acquisition, graph construction, native generation/load/readback/query work, remaining full checks, inference, certification, persistence, monitoring, and total elapsed time.

Keep logical time distinct from processing time. Equal model loss does not establish equal real-time responsiveness.

Keep nested cost timers separate. Do not add them as disjoint phases. Wire-byte totals, source payload, RSS, peak memory, and publication size remain different quantities; report unmeasured quantities as unmeasured.

Do not aggregate away unfavorable parents or declare superiority from retrieval parity. Provide sequence explanations for unresolved or surprising results, without converting accounting decompositions into causal claims that were not tested.

## 9. Deliverables

Reuse existing compare/audit/archive conventions. Deliver:

1. One preregistered fixture manifest with source-bound controller and evaluator identities.
2. One command that runs the twenty-four executions and writes the normal traces and report.
3. The applicable regression receipt and explicit omitted coverage.
4. One source-bound review retaining positive, neutral, adverse, unavailable, and failed cases; one independently checkable archive/replay record using the existing tooling.
5. A short research summary distinguishing implemented capability, contract conformance, task effectiveness, and hypotheses still unsupported.

Do not build a new archive format, audit language, dashboard, deployment framework, or provenance subsystem. Add only the test/input plumbing actually needed for this cohort.

## 10. Stop condition

Stop after the frozen evaluation and its report, regardless of the completion fraction or timing.

The milestone does not require better outcomes than the old fixtures, native speed over scanning, a pressure advantage, or all tasks completing. It requires honest, reproducible evaluation under the declared information and resource limits.

Do not automatically continue into:

- Another native-runtime or payload optimization.
- Persistent/incremental AtomSpace, helper pooling, cross-view caching, selective invalidation, or altered integrity checks.
- New pressure scores, learned conductance, adaptive transport, or a new scheduler.
- More inference depth, probability calibration, evidence supersession, threshold changes, or policy-B promotion.
- Generalized recovery, new physical-world behavior, or an unbounded benchmark expansion.

After publication, select any future change from an observed cognitive limitation and a separate hypothesis. Do not make obtaining a win for a preferred mechanism the target.
