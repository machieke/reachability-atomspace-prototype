# Decision and intermediate parity

The frozen scanner independently reconstructs every work graph at the native
run's exact public snapshot. A persistent copy of the unchanged consumer replays
priority, age, exact premises, selections, stop reasons and cumulative budgets.
Each native query's IDs are separately checked by the old brute-force source
reference. The audit then cold-loads the same snapshots and reexecutes native
queries, checking raw protocol lines, membership, binding and complete graph.
No reference answer enters the executing controller.

Closed-loop pairs separately compare each tick/slot, selected kind/target/exact
premises, reply, observed summary, budget and stop. Only the candidate's opaque
cross-authority basis and expected-binding hashes are removed for this comparison;
within each run these are checked exactly. Inputs, lineage, intermediate IDs,
logical IDs, cost, tie keys and outcomes remain exact.

Two-hop and three-hop use two and three actual formula calls respectively.
Shared work uses four calls but longest dependency depth three: one committed
intermediate is referenced by two applications. Replacement uses three calls and
current depth two; the old intermediate is retired, and equal new numbers require
new exact bindings. No intermediate is seeded. Unavailable input is requested
once and remains unresolved. Adverse evidence computes three calls but prevents
new deployment through complete review and frozen authority.

All four successful parents dispatch at logical tick zero, because their work
fits the frozen eight-slot allowance; independent effects appear at tick one and
admitted completion at tick three. J_world and J_certified are both 30. Unavailable
and adverse parents retain loss 10 and integrals 90. The same results hold in all
retrieval/formula combinations. No first-divergence causal attribution is needed:
there are no supported semantic choice divergences in these paired trajectories.
The native query/load overhead is real wall time even where logical outcomes tie.
